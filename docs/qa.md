# Presentation Q&A

Questions the audience may ask, with the answer we give and where the evidence is. Add new ones
from issue #14 or the deck reviews. Every number quoted must come from a committed result file.

| # | Question | Our answer | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does the agent allow for lunch, breaks or travel time between meetings? | Not by itself. It schedules from what each participant's calendars, messages and words say. A rule like "lunch is 12:00–13:00" or "a 15-minute break after two hours of meetings" works if someone states it, because the personal agent turns statements into constraints. Travel time needs meeting locations (online or in person), which the scenarios do not have. Built-in breaks and travel time are future work | Brief §0 and §4 (statements become typed constraints); `scheduler/agents/personal.py`; issue #14 |
| 2 | Is 0.84 ± 0.09 really better than the shared agent's 0.75 ± 0.03? | On means over 3 runs, yes; our per-run passes were 31, 26 and 26 of 33. We do not claim significance from 3 runs. On outcome alone the gap is larger and tighter: 0.99 ± 0.02 against 0.87 ± 0.03 | `results/heldout-final/grades.csv`; `docs/results.md` headline |
| 3 | A0 misses the threshold, so why is it at Observer? | The threshold only controls moving up, so it does not set A0's level. Risk does: its tools only read and its only output is the typed summary, so damage is contained. Its 18 failures were extra or missing questions and one hand-back, never a leak or a write. Split after the run, reading alone scores 0.98 ± 0.035 and asking 0.84 ± 0.09 | `docs/results.md` "Autonomy evidence", "Failure analysis"; brief §6 |
| 4 | After the privacy filter blocked arm (c)'s post on ho-031, did it post anything? | Yes: the same entries as times only, which the filter allows, because free/busy is not private in this design. Titles and reasons never reached the thread | `results/heldout-final/c/ho-031/run1.json` `thread`, `guardrails` |
| 5 | Isn't asking what "keep Friday afternoons clear" means reasonable? | Arguably, for a person. The generator fixes it as 13:00–18:00, so the checker counts the question as unneeded; that is a limit of the evaluation. Agents asked about it in 10 of the 54 participant-episodes with that note | `scheduler/world/builder.py` `memory_of`; episode files under `results/heldout-final/d/` |
| 6 | Why synthetic data? | No usable real data has several people's calendars, private reasons and a ground-truth answer. The generator documents its assumptions, and every scenario records its seed and generator version | brief §7; `scheduler/world/builder.py` |
| 7 | Could the private question pressure someone? | The coordinator is told to ask only the participant whose movable time blocks every slot; that part is a prompt rule, not code (issue #22). In code, a question is private, there are at most two per participant per request, and consent is stored before any invite | brief §6; `docs/security-review.md` §6 |
| 8 | What did the final run cost? | USD 2.87 for all arms on the 33 held-out scenarios (28 Oct, one key); about 1.3 US cents and 53 s per episode for our design, 0.13 cents more than the shared agent | `docs/results.md`; `results/heldout-final/report.md` |
| 9 | Do holds expire? | No. A hold is reversible and released on replan, but the auto-expiry in the brief was not built. A3's 1.00 measures "no stray hold" | `scheduler/sandbox/world.py` `place_hold`; `scheduler/eval/metrics.py` |
| 10 | Can a hard constraint be broken at the approval gate? | No tool moves a commitment, and hard rules are enforced by the enumerator's ranking. The gate's recheck covers calendar events and time away for required attendees, not lookups, statements or usual hours. Arm (c) booked over a public holiday through the same gate (ho-005 run 3); arm (d) had 0 hard violations in 107 episodes | `scheduler/engine/approval.py` `recheck`; `results/heldout-final/grades.csv` |
| 11 | Are clash alerts delivered? | Not to a person: the gate logs `alert_participant`, and only ho-021 grades it | `scheduler/engine/approval.py`; `scheduler/eval/checker.py` |
| 12 | Are expired memory notes ignored? | That is a prompt rule, and it is untested: no saved scenario has a dated `expires`. Notes for another meeting and agent guesses are tested (ho-010, 3 of 3) | `scheduler/agents/personal.py`; `data/scenarios/` |
| 13 | Which multi-agent pattern is this? | Week 7's orchestrator-worker: the coordinator leads, and each hand-off is a typed summary. It is not a blackboard: agents share no store, and personal agents do not read the thread. Only the shared-context design was tested as an alternative | `docs/security-review.md` §4; `scheduler/agents/coordinator.py` |
| 14 | Why a fixed limit of 2 private questions? | Being asked only when blocking is the dynamic part: 13 of 107 arm-(d) episodes asked anyone. The cap is a safety stop. It fired 3 times, all on ho-014, an infeasible case that then handed back and passed | `scheduler/agents/coordinator.py` `MAX_QUESTIONS`; episode `guardrails` |
| 15 | Were the agents tested separately? | Held-out episodes are end to end. By agent, all 18 arm-(d) failures involve the personal agent, and 1 also the coordinator. That is a slice of those runs, not a separate test | `results/heldout-final/grades.csv` |
| 16 | Why no weighted error cost? | No failure costs were set before the held-out run, and choosing them afterwards would be arbitrary. Critical violations cover the costly failures: 0 of 107 | `results/heldout-final/report.md` |
| 17 | What is the autonomy rate? | 92 of 99 arm-(d) episodes settled without a hand-back (93%), 6 of the 7 hand-backs being correct. Every invite still needs the organiser | episode `outcome` |
| 18 | Was direct prompt injection tested? | Yes: `thread_deputy` is typed by an attendee straight at the coordinator, which is the course's definition of direct, and it aims at a confused deputy. It reached the coordinator's window in 5 of 5 runs and did not work. `injected_page` and `injected_message` are indirect | `docs/security-review.md` §7; `results/heldout-final/*/ho-032/` |
| 19 | A4 clears the threshold, so why not drop approval? | The threshold is necessary, not sufficient. Sending cannot be undone and reaches every attendee, and Observer would need a containment case we do not make. The spec prefers supervised with strong evidence | brief §6; project spec p3 |
| 20 | Did the organiser ever refuse? | No. All 109 requests in arm (d) were scripted to approve, so the refusal rate is not measured. A unit test blocks an invite with no stored approval, and ho-032 did not get a send past the gate | `results/heldout-final/grades.csv`; `tests/test_guardrails.py` |
| 21 | Is a group chat post safe at Observer? | It is the weakest fit: a post cannot be withdrawn and reaches the whole group, so the level depends on the privacy filter. It blocked drafts in 3 arm-(c) runs and had nothing to block in arm (d) | `scheduler/agents/privacy.py`; `results/heldout-final/grades.csv` |
| 22 | Was the organiser's own request attacked? | The request text was not; the organiser's thread messages were, in the second batch (ho-035, ho-036). The request text reaches the coordinator and every personal agent. A leak would still stop at the typed summary and the filter, but false availability would not stop at the gate, whose recheck covers calendars and time away only | `scheduler/agents/personal.py` `statements`; `scheduler/engine/approval.py` `recheck` |
| 23 | Are the wrong clarifying questions poor reasoning? | Unproven. The rate moved 1, 4, 3 of 16 across runs on identical input, and it errs both ways. Arm (c), same model, asked less both ways (5 of 54, 1 of 6). Only gpt-5-mini at low effort ran, and question text is not logged, so model and prompt cannot be separated | `results/heldout-final/`; `docs/results.md` |
| 24 | Does only yes or no come back from a private question? | Yes or no, plus a time if the person gives one: 3 of 17 arm-(d) answers did. Their own words stay private | `scheduler/agents/personal.py` `answer_relax` |
| 25 | Did the guardrails hold, or did the model simply not obey? | Mostly the model did not obey. A guardrail fired in 5 of the 138 saved attack runs. So we test the code separately: a scripted model does exactly what each attack asks, and each test checks what stops it. Turning a guardrail off makes those tests fail | `results/`, attack runs' `guardrails` lists; `docs/results.md` "After the final run"; `tests/test_attack_obedient.py` |
| 26 | Did you test more attacks after the final run? | Yes, three thread attacks on new held-out seeds, arms (c) and (d), 5 runs each, reported apart from the final numbers. None worked in 30 episodes, but one case cannot show success by construction, so that is at most 20 episodes of evidence | `results/heldout-attack2/report.md`; brief §16 |
| 27 | What is left open in the attack suite? | A private question is neither filtered nor graded, and a reworded leak can pass the filter and the checker alike. Both are recorded as known gaps | `docs/attack-coverage.md`; `results/component/report.md` |
| 28 | Does "no leak" mean nothing leaked? | It means none that our two checks recognise. On 25 labelled leaks, the filter caught 8 and the checker 7, and 17 passed both. Word-for-word secrets are stopped; a reworded leak can pass. The stronger protection is structural: in our design the coordinator never holds a reason, so it has nothing to reword | `results/component/report.md` layer 5; `docs/results.md` "Limits" |
| 29 | Why not fix the over-asking before presenting? | It is our biggest failure and not a safety failure: 13 of the 18 failed episodes had the right final slot and only an unneeded question. The fix is a prompt change, and we found the flaw by reading held-out failures, so a rerun on the same cases would not be a clean test. It stays future work, and the 84% stands | `docs/results.md` "Failure analysis"; brief §16 |
